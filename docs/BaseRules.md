# Guess the link Gift Exchange Rules

## Setup

### Before the event

Each player buys the following:

1. Two (2) Gifts priced around $15 for a total value of at least $30
2. Two (2) Brown target paper bags, one for each gift.
2. One (1) Gift card of the player's choice valued around $10 for the winner's pot

The gifts must be "linked" in some way. For example, a baseball and a basketball could be
"linked" because they're both balls for sports. A harder example would be a deck of cards
and a pan set with the link being "Grandma's Hobbies". The goal is to split the room, with
the max points awarded to those who get around 50% of players to guess the link.

### Player Arrival

Each Gift and gift bag needs to be marked with a number. Numbers help for the following
reasons:

1. Allow players to descretely avoid choosing their own gift.
2. Help handle the case where two players bring identical gifts.
3. Allows a player-GM to identify two "linked" gifts without compromising fairness.

Randomly generate numbers between 1-100. Write these down on pieces of paper and
place them in a hat.

When players arrive, have them draw one number for each gift out of the hat.
Only the player should know which number their gifts were assigned. If the GM is
a player, a system is required that will prevent the GM from linking the numbers
to the players. Otherwise, a non-player GM is allowed to know. The number should be
visible on the bag, but hideable until gametime. This can be done by marking the
outside of the bag near the top and folding the bag over the number.

Once the gifts are marked, the gifts should be placed together either in the play
area or a staging room. Ensure that players cannot deduce the player who brought
a gift based on factors like arrival time or placement in the play area.

### Gametime

Have players sit in a circle. Place all gifts in the center of the circle. Once
all gifts are placed, reveal the numbers marked on each gift. Instruct all players
to grab a single gift and return to their seats. While it's allowed to posses your
own gift, you may encourage the players to avoid choosing gifts marked with
their own numbers.

## Play

Play starts by randomly selecting someone in the circle to go first. Then this
player will take an exchange turn (explained below). Once the exchange turn is
completed, the next player clockwise in the circle takes their turn.

### Exchange Turns

Exchange turns are simple. The player rolls a d6 and takes an action according
to the dice as listed below:

1. Take/Exchange Gift from middle (max two gifts per player)
2. Trade gifts with player on left. Each player chooses their own gift to give.
3. Trade gifts with player on right. Each player chooses their own gift to give.
4. Trade gifts with any player. Choose the gift you want.
5. Make another player unwrap a wrapped gift they posses.
6. Steal any gift (other player replenishes from middle).

To ensure everyone leaves with something, each player may only possess 1 or 2
gifts at any time. If anyone loses all their gifts for any reason, they should
take a gift from the middle or request a consentual "handout" from any player.

#### 1. Take/Exchange Gift from middle

Player takes a gift from the middle. If the player has two (2) gifts already,
they must choose a gift (wrapped or unwrapped) to place in the middle.
This action must be taken.

#### 2 - 3. Trade gifts with player on left/right

Player must choose a gift to give to the player on the left/right. In exchange,
the player on the left/right must choose a gift to give back in return.
This action must be taken.

#### 4. Force trade gifts with any player

Player chooses a give to give to and a gift to receive from a target player of
their choice.
This action may be passed.

#### 5. Make another player unwrap a wrapped gift they posess.

Player chooses any player with a wrapped gift. Target player must unwrap the chosen
gift. If all posessed gifts are unwrapped, the player may unwrap a gift in the middle
and return it to the center. If all gifts are unwrapped, the action is passed.
This action may be passed.

#### 6. Steal any gift

Player chooses any gift and gains posession of it. If player posesses two gifts,
they must choose one to part with and return to the middle. If the gift is taken
from a player who is left with no gifts, the targeted player must replenish their
gift from the middle or accept a consensual handout. If no gifts remain in the
middle, this action becomes a forced trade. If the chosen gift is still wrapped,
unwrap it.
This action may be passed.

#### Additional Actions

After taking a dice action, you may opt to do an additional action. If you pass
any passable dice action, you may take two actions. Additional Actions are as follows:

1. Unwrap a gift in your posession.
2. Guess who brought a gift in your posession (must not be previously revealed). If correct, you gain a lock item. You also gain a finders fee of 100.
3. Guess if two unwrapped gifts are linked (must not be previously revealed). One of the gifts must be in your posession. If correct you either gain a lock item or may steal the other gift. You also gain a finders fee of 200.
4. Use a lock item on a possessed gift. The target gift now may not be unconsensually stolen except by a super steal. If given away or traded, the lock travels with the gift.
5. **Secretly** guess the link between two gifts publicly revealed to be linked (publicly through either actions 2 or 3). If exactly correct (see link guessing section), the player gains double link points and
may choose to super steal one or both of the linked items. A super steal is the only steal that bypasses lock items. If the items were locked, the affected player(s) gains a lock item per stolen item. The
player receives a lock item for each linked item they opt not to steal, which may be applied immediately without using an action. If the guess is incorrect, the guess becomes public knowledge.

## Ending the Exchange Phase

The current round of play becomes the penultimate round when all gifts are unwrapped
and no no gifts remain in the center. The penultimate round finishes normally until
it is the first player's turn, which marks the beginning of the final round. Every
player takes one more turn, ending with the player counter-clockwise to the starting
player. Once the exchange phase is complete, play moves to the link guessing phase.

## Guessing Phase

The link guessing phase is where most link guessing points will be earned.

### Take Inventory of items

Make a item list for each of the following categories:

1. Unlinked items
2. Link but no giver (can happen if a player guesses that the items are linked before an author is revealed)
3. Link and known giver

Each item in play must fall into one of the above categories.

Remember only the giver of an item and the fact that two items are linked should
be public at this point. **The exact way in which the items are linked must still
be a secret** except to the giver and to a player who guessed the item link exactly correctly.

### Link Association Guessing Round

Start with the items in list 1. Ask each player to secretly submit (with their name)
what items in that list are linked to other unlinked items. The GM may display or clarify
which items it may possibly be linked to. If a giver for any item was guessed, it should
also be displayed or clarified. Players should not provide a guess for items they brought.

Once all the responses are collected, reveal the correct the link associations for each item.
Award 200 points for all correct link association guesses. Add the items to list 2 or 3
based on whether the items now have known givers.

### Giver Guessing Round

Now take all the linked items with unknown givers in list 2. Again, ask each player to secretly submit (with their name)
who they think brought each item pair. Once all the responses are collected, the correct givers are
revealed. Award correct guesses with 200 points. If no one is able to correctly guess the giver of a pair of items,
the giver is awarded 100 points per opponent. Add all items to list 3, which should now contain all items.

### Link Guessing Rounds

Now for the finale. Take list 3. Going through each item pair individually, have everyone
secretly submit a guess for what the link is. Hand all these responses to the giver, who will
secretly decide which responses are exactly correct. The giver will hand each exactly correct
response back to the player, awarding them 100 points. Each correct respondent will have the
chance to resubmit an incorrect response. The giver receives 200 points times the number of correct
guesses minus 200 times the distance from splitting the room. For example, in a game of 13 players,
if 6 players guess correctly, the giver is awarded 1200. If 5 or 7 players guess correctly,
the giver is awarded 1000, and so on. If there is an odd number of opponents, add the giver
as a correct guess for this calculation.

Once all incorrect responses are collected, the giver will insert one correct response.
The GM will then shuffle and read each of these responses allowed. Everyone has
one chance to secretly guess which link is correct out of the responses. Each correct response
is 100 points. Each player who has their answer chosen by another player receives 50 points
per player, including the giver. If all players manage to guess the link exactly, all players
receive 100 points and this round is skipped. If preferred, players who guessed the link
exactly may skip the round, automatically being awarded an additional 100 points.

Repeat these rounds until all is revealed.

### Final tally and awards

Add up all the scores from guessing. Declare first, second, and third place winners.
1st place receives 5/8ths of the gift cards in the pot, 2nd place receives 2/8ths,
and 3rd place receives 1/8ths. Have 1st place select 3/8ths of the pot as their reserve.
Next, have 2nd place select all of their gift cards, followed by 3rd place. Finally,
give the remaining gift cards to the winner.
